# Hook bank

The first three seconds decide everything else. These are written to be said
out loud, flat, with no windup.

Test: **would this make you stop if it weren't yours?** If you hesitate, it's a
no.

---

## Tested-shape hooks — a claim with a number

- "We cut our posting by 60% and our reach went up."
- "47% of our inbound came from six posts. We published 180."
- "I asked ChatGPT for a LinkedIn post. It scored 28 out of 100."
- "Our best post last year was 41 words."
- "Three words are killing your reach and you type them every week."
- "We deleted our content calendar in March. Inbound doubled."

## Contrarian — argue with the received wisdom

- "Posting every day is the worst advice in this industry."
- "Your engagement rate is a vanity metric too."
- "'Just be authentic' is the least actionable advice ever given."
- "Content calendars aren't strategy. They're compliance documents."
- "The algorithm was never your problem."

## Callout — name the reader's situation

- "If your posts sound like AI, it isn't the AI's fault."
- "You're not short on ideas. You're short on the nerve to publish less."
- "You've been optimising the 219 posts that did nothing."
- "Every scheduler helps you post more. None of them tell you what to post."

## Curiosity gap — only use when the payoff is real

- "One of these posts I wrote. One an AI wrote. Can you tell?"
- "I scored every post I published last year. The pattern surprised me."
- "There's a number that predicts whether your post will work. Here it is."

## Demonstration — the screen is the hook

- *(silent, just the score falling to 29)* "…that's what AI writing scores."
- *(typing, score climbing live)* "Watch the number."

---

## Hooks that do not work

Learn these so you stop reaching for them:

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| "Hey guys, in this video…" | Two seconds of nothing. They're gone. |
| "Let me show you our new tool" | Announces an ad. |
| "Content marketing is more important than ever" | Says nothing. Could open any video. |
| "Introducing Postwave" | Nobody has any reason to care yet. |
| "Are you struggling with social media?" | Infomercial cadence. |

---

## Writing your own

1. **Lead with the number.** "60%" beats "significantly."
2. **Make it arguable.** If nobody could disagree, nobody will comment.
3. **Under twelve words.** Say it aloud. If you need a breath, cut it.
4. **No setup.** Delete the first sentence you write. It's almost always a windup.
5. **Be specific enough to be falsifiable.** "Six posts drove 47% of inbound" is
   credible precisely because it's oddly exact.
