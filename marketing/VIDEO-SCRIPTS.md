# Short-form video scripts

Five scripts, ordered by how likely they are to travel. Each is built for
9:16, 15–40 seconds, watchable with sound off.

**Rules that apply to all five:**

- The first frame is not a logo. It's the thing that makes someone stop.
- No intro. No "hey guys." The first word is the hook.
- Screen recording at 2× speed, except the moment the score changes — that plays
  at 1×, because it's the payoff.
- Burned-in captions always. Most views are muted.
- The product name appears once, at the end, for one second.

---

## 1. "I asked AI to write my LinkedIn post"

**The strongest one. Lead with this.** It works because everyone has felt the
cringe of AI-written LinkedIn slop, and the video punishes it on screen.

| Time | Shot | On screen | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Screen recording: pasting an obviously AI-written post into the composer | *(the post itself is the visual)* | "I asked ChatGPT for a LinkedIn post." |
| 0:03–0:06 | The score panel resolves. **Hold on the number. 1× speed.** Red **29**. | `29 / 100` | "Then I ran it through a scorer." |
| 0:06–0:12 | Slow scroll down the six signals. Linger on Hook **5** and Algorithm risk. | `Hook — 5` · `generic AI-sounding opener` | "Hook: five out of a hundred. Generic AI opener. Seven hashtags. Engagement bait." |
| 0:12–0:16 | Cursor moves to **Make it sound like me**. Click. | `Make it sound like me` | "So I trained it on ten posts I'd actually written." |
| 0:16–0:21 | Text rewrites. Score climbs **28 → 87**. **1× speed. Do not cut this.** | `87 / 100` `Voice match 89%` | "Same idea. My words." |
| 0:21–0:25 | Split-screen: before and after, side by side | `Before` / `After` | "One of these sounds like a person." |
| 0:25–0:27 | Logo, one second | `Postwave` | — |

**Why it works:** the before/after is unfakeable and takes zero explanation.
The viewer does the judging themselves.

**Cut to 15s:** drop rows 3 and 6.

## 2. "Your scheduler has no idea if your post is good"

The category-attack video. Names the competitor set without naming a company.

| Time | Shot | On screen | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Fast montage: a generic scheduling calendar, posts dragged onto days | `Schedule` `Schedule` `Schedule` | "Every scheduler does the same thing. It puts your post in a queue." |
| 0:04–0:07 | Cut to black. Text only. | **"None of them tell you if it's any good."** | "None of them tell you if it's any good." |
| 0:07–0:13 | Composer, typing a real post. Score updates live as the words land. | `71` → `78` → `84` | "This one scores it while you write. Hook. Readability. Length for the platform. Whether the algorithm will bury it." |
| 0:13–0:19 | Cut to the analytics calibration chart. Dots, dashed line. | `Is the score working?` | "And it checks its own work. Every dot is a post it predicted, against how it actually did." |
| 0:19–0:24 | Cut to "What your audience rewards", bars with green deltas | `Hook strength 34% +4` | "After a few weeks it stops guessing and starts learning what *your* audience rewards." |
| 0:24–0:26 | Logo | `Postwave` | — |

**Why it works:** shipping the chart that could prove you wrong reads as
confidence. That's the whole emotional beat.

## 3. "The three words killing your reach"

**Most likely to be stolen and reposted.** That's fine — that's reach.

| Time | Shot | On screen | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | Close on the composer. Type: `In today's fast-paced world` | *(typing)* | "Three things that are quietly killing your reach." |
| 0:02–0:05 | Hook score drops on screen as the words appear | `Hook 5` `generic AI-sounding opener` | "One. The windup. Nobody read past it." |
| 0:05–0:09 | Paste a link into the body. Algorithm-risk meter goes red. | `outbound link — LinkedIn suppresses reach` | "Two. A link in the post. LinkedIn buries it. Put it in the first comment." |
| 0:09–0:13 | Add seven hashtags. Meter drops further. | `7 hashtags — LinkedIn wants at most 3` | "Three. Hashtag soup. That's a 2019 move." |
| 0:13–0:17 | Delete all three. Score climbs — but only to **40**. Hold on it. | `28` → `40` | "Fix all three and it still only scores forty." |
| 0:17–0:22 | Cut to the Hook row, still red at 5. Then rewrite the first line. Score jumps. | `Hook 5` → `Hook 81` · `82` | "Because none of those were the real problem. The first line was." |
| 0:22–0:24 | Logo | `Postwave` | — |

**Why it works:** it's genuinely useful even to someone who never installs
anything, and the twist at 0:17 is what makes it shareable — the video sets up
three tips and then tells you they were the small stuff. Saves and shares come
from utility plus a turn, not from wanting the product.

> These numbers are real and verified against the seeded demo account. They
> drift by a point or two as the predictor retrains on new posts — re-check them
> before you record and adjust the on-screen text if needed.

## 4. "I deleted my content calendar"

Founder-POV, the most "organic" of the five. Film this one on a phone, talking,
with the screen recording cut in.

| Time | Shot | On screen | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Talking head, no intro, mid-thought | — | "We cut our posting from four a week to two, and our reach went up." |
| 0:04–0:08 | Screen: the calendar, then posts disappearing | — | "The calendar was never scheduling content. It was scheduling obligation." |
| 0:08–0:14 | Screen: the queue filling with pre-scored drafts | `Autopilot · 5 drafts awaiting approval` | "Now there's a queue. It writes a week ahead, in my voice, and scores everything before I see it." |
| 0:14–0:19 | Approving a draft with one click. It lands in a slot. | `Approved → Tue 16:30` | "I approve the good ones. The weak ones just don't go out." |
| 0:19–0:23 | Talking head | — | "Turns out we were never short on ideas. We were short on the nerve to publish less." |
| 0:23–0:25 | Logo | `Postwave` | — |

**Why it works:** the contrarian claim ("we posted less and grew") is the hook.
The product is almost incidental, which is why it doesn't read as an ad.

## 5. "Can AI write like you? Test it."

Interactive framing. Highest comment rate of the five — people argue in replies.

| Time | Shot | On screen | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Two posts, side by side, unlabelled | `A` / `B` | "One of these I wrote. One an AI wrote, trained on ten of my posts." |
| 0:03–0:08 | Hold on both. Let people read. **Do not talk over this.** | `A` / `B` | *(silence — let them read)* |
| 0:08–0:12 | Reveal the Voice Match panel on each | `A — 91%` `B — 88%` | "Three points apart." |
| 0:12–0:17 | Scroll the fingerprint readout: sentence length, vocabulary | `13.4 words/sentence` `24% of your words` | "It measured how I write. Sentence length. The words I actually reach for. The ones I never use." |
| 0:17–0:21 | Back to the two posts | **"Which one was the AI?"** | "Guess in the comments. Answer's pinned." |
| 0:21–0:23 | Logo | `Postwave` | — |

**Why it works:** it asks a question the viewer wants to answer publicly. Pin
the answer, reply to the first fifty guesses — comment velocity in the first
hour is what most algorithms actually reward.

## Production notes

**Length.** Aim 15–25s. Anything over 35s needs a second hook around 0:12 or
retention collapses.

**Pacing.** Cut on every sentence. If a shot holds longer than 3 seconds, it had
better be the score changing.

**Speed ramping.** Typing and scrolling at 2×. The moment the number changes:
1×, sometimes 0.75×. That contrast *is* the edit.

**Sound.** Trending audio low under the VO. One clean UI "tick" when the score
lands — a single sound effect on the payoff frame is worth more than a music
bed.

**Captions.** Burned in, high contrast, 2–4 words per card, bottom third. Never
rely on the platform's auto-captions.

**Don't** show a pricing page, say "revolutionary", show a logo before 0:20, or
explain the architecture. Nobody is watching a 20-second video to learn about
your regression weights.

## Shooting order

Record **one** screen-capture session covering every shot, then cut five videos
out of it. The setup cost is entirely in getting the app into the right state,
and [RECORDING-SETUP.md](RECORDING-SETUP.md) gets you there once.
