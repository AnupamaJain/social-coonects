# Captions, CTAs and cadence

The video does the work. The caption's only job is to add the context the video
couldn't carry, and to give people a reason to comment.

---

## Per platform

### TikTok / Reels / Shorts

Short. One line of context, one question. No links in the caption — they
suppress reach, and on TikTok they aren't clickable anyway.

> I ran an AI-written LinkedIn post through a scorer. 28 out of 100.
>
> Then I trained it on ten posts I'd actually written. 87.
>
> What's the worst AI-written post you've seen this week?

Hashtags: 3–5, specific, no generic soup.
`#linkedintips #b2bmarketing #contentstrategy #aiwriting`

### X

The video autoplays muted, so captions are load-bearing. Thread the detail
rather than cramming it into one post.

> Post 1: I asked ChatGPT for a LinkedIn post.
> It scored 28/100. [video]
>
> Post 2: The scorer checks six things: hook, readability, CTA, length for the
> platform, algorithm risk, and whether it sounds like you.
> The AI version failed five of them.
>
> Post 3: Then I trained it on 10 posts I'd actually written.
> Same idea. My words. 87/100.
>
> Post 4: Built this because every scheduler helps you post *more*, and none of
> them tell you whether what you're posting is any good.
> [link]

Link goes in the **last** post, never the first.

### LinkedIn

Longer, first-person, a real opinion. The video is supporting evidence, not the
point. Link in the first comment — LinkedIn suppresses posts with outbound
links, which is exactly what the product flags.

> We cut our posting from four times a week to twice, and our reach went up.
>
> I don't think that's a paradox. We were publishing four mediocre posts a week
> because the calendar said to, and the two decent ones were paying for the two
> that weren't.
>
> The thing that changed it wasn't discipline. It was having a number to argue
> with. Before anything goes out now, it gets scored: hook, readability, length
> for the platform, whether the algorithm will bury it, and whether it actually
> sounds like me.
>
> The weak drafts don't get killed. They just don't go out on a Tuesday because
> it's Tuesday.
>
> What would you stop publishing if you had to halve your output?

## CTAs, weakest to strongest

| CTA | Use when |
|---|---|
| *(none)* | Default. Early on, reach matters more than clicks |
| "Guess in the comments, answer's pinned" | Script 5. Highest comment rate |
| "What would you drop first?" | Any of them. Invites a real opinion |
| "Link in bio if you want to try it" | Only after the video has earned it |
| "Comment SCORE and I'll send it" | Works, but reads as a growth hack. Use sparingly |

**Never** "like and share", "tag a friend", or "smash that follow". The product
literally flags those as engagement bait — getting caught using them is an
avoidable own goal.

---

## Cadence

**Weeks 1–2 — find the hook.** All five scripts, one every other day. Change
nothing but the hook. You're testing openings, not content.

**Weeks 3–4 — double down.** Whichever hook shape won, write four more in that
shape. Reshoot the winner with a tighter edit.

**Ongoing — 3 a week.** Two in the proven shape, one experiment. Kill anything
below your median 3-second retention.

## What to actually measure

1. **3-second retention.** The only number that grades the hook. Below ~50% and
   nothing downstream matters.
2. **Average watch percentage.** Grades the edit.
3. **Comments per view.** Grades whether you gave people something to argue with.
4. **Saves.** The best proxy for "this was useful," which is what gets reshared.

Follower count is the last thing to look at and the first thing everyone looks
at. Views without saves or comments are noise.

## Reusing this for the product itself

Post these with Postwave. Run the captions through the scorer first.

If your own launch copy scores 40, that's the product telling you something,
and fixing it before publishing is the most honest demo you'll ever run.
